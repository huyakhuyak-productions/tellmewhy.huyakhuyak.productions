tellmewhy·desktop directions
Focused column

The phone experience, made dignified — one comfortable reading column (~68ch) floating on a quiet, glowing canvas. Conversations collapse into a slide-over so nothing competes with the words. Honest tradeoff: it wastes horizontal space and hides history one click away, which power users may find slow.

Journal spread

The familiar messenger shape, restyled as a private journal: a persistent left rail reads like a table of contents with dated entries, chat on the right. Fast to move between sessions. Tradeoff: the two-pane frame is the most conventional here, and the rail is always visible even when you'd rather forget the backlog.

Companion desk

Three zones — history, chat, and a quiet right column reserved for what phase 2/3 brings: a licensed-listener review line, a mood trend, notes to your future self. It sells the roadmap without shipping it. Tradeoff: the most to build and the easiest to make feel clinical if the placeholders ever get loud.

Evening room

The risk. Generous negative space with the conversation anchored low, like a fireside chat, and the composer as the visual anchor you're drawn to. Calmest and most distinct. Tradeoff: unconventional scrolling, less at-a-glance density, and it only holds up with real restraint — easy to break.

Letters

Not a chat at all — correspondence. The exchange reads as alternating letter blocks on one continuous page: their letters in full serif, yours indented with a quiet rule; the composer is a writing desk at the page's end. Tradeoff: gorgeous for slow, reflective exchange, but it fights the streaming, back-and-forth reality of the product and would feel wrong for quick check-ins.

Timeline river

No conversations at all — one continuous stream across days, with date nodes and mood marks inline; sessions are just eddies in the river, and you navigate by scrubbing time on the left. Tradeoff: the truest picture of an ongoing relationship with the practice, but it buries "find that one conversation" and needs mood data the product doesn't capture yet.

Card garden

The landing is the experience: a calm bento of tiles — start talking, recent entries, mood, a note left for you — and the chat opens as a centered sheet floating over the garden. Tradeoff: the best overview-first shape and a natural roadmap home, but the sheet re-miniaturizes the chat you came to have, and modal chat can feel interruptible.

Guided session

Closest to how a session actually feels and least like a chat app: one question at a time, large and centered, your answer beneath, everything prior collapsed into a ribbon of small markers above. Tradeoff: powerful focus and zero interface noise, but it constrains free-form venting — it presumes the AI leads, which isn't always what a hard night needs.

Composite — the ship direction

The chosen hybrid, two screens (toggle top-center). Home: a Guided-style prompt leads, a few recent entries support. Conversation: Desk's frame with both rails visually receded, and a Journal center rebuilt for long-form reading — long AI reflections render as serif passages, long user reflections as tinted panels, short exchanges stay bubbles. Watch: the passage/bubble threshold needs a real rule in implementation (~280 chars or 2+ paragraphs), or the thread will feel inconsistent.
